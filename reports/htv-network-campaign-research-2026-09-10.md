# HTV Network: campaign strategy and public account inventory

Research date: 10 September 2026. Source video: CBC News, [“Exposing a global AI slop empire ruining the internet for profit”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ), published 9 September 2026, duration 6:51. Reviewed using the watch skill: full available captions, 28 sampled frames, 19 timestamp-specific frames, and three additional 1080p frames.

CBC/Radio-Canada attributes the operation to HTV Network in Hanoi, led by Ngo Anh Tan. He denied that the pages and sites belonged to HTV. The company is distinct from the Vietnamese broadcaster with a similar name. These are the investigation’s findings, not an independent ownership audit. [Company identification, 2:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=158s); [response, 5:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=336s).

CBC’s published investigation reports close to 200 Facebook pages, more than 55 million combined followers, and about 50 connected websites. Combined followers should not be interpreted as unique people. Those totals are reproduced in [this indexed account of the investigation](https://www.newsminimalist.com/articles/vietnamese-firm-runs-fake-ai-news-network-targeting-canadians-probe-finds-e17c650f). The separate June 22 [Media Ecosystem Observatory report](https://mediatechdemocracy.com/en/publications/facebook-news-slop-economy_2026/) studies 340 coordinated pages; its entire network should not be assigned to HTV.

The business is sustained publishing for advertising income. Its observable flow is: **attention-grabbing Facebook content → article links → advertising-filled websites → revenue**. The video also discusses possible platform payments, but does not establish eligibility or earnings for every page. [1:28–1:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=88s).

| Strategy | Evidence and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Many audience-facing brands | Sports, entertainment, family stories, and general-news pages provide different entry points into connected websites. Several pages share one destination; individual pages can also link to several domains. This structure is visible in the reporters’ spreadsheet at [4:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=268s). |
| Fast emotional hooks | The examples use urgent framing, dramatic imagery, celebrity names, tragedy, and religious conflict to attract attention. CBC identifies fabricated claims as well as ordinary clickbait; these categories should not be conflated. [1:00–1:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=60s). |
| Convert feed attention into website visits | Links appear in post text and comments. The video visibly connects Nhạc Rap Việt to tinfilmus.com, Life is Colorful and Just For You to autulu.com, and I Love Rugby - Kelce to btuatu.com. [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=62s), [1:09–1:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=69s), [3:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=223s). |
| Reusable site presentation | Several sites show the same “News Insider” presentation. A truck-sales homepage is shown alongside a separate news-style section. Repeated design supports a shared production process, although design similarity alone would not prove common ownership. [2:14–3:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=134s). |
| Central staff and localization | Recruitment material assigns staff English- and Spanish-language news sites for American and European markets, monitoring trends, posting through an existing article system, and managing assigned Facebook pages. The job advertisement also mentions CapCut experience as advantageous. [4:04–4:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=244s). |
| Separate corporate identity from public-facing pages | CBC’s attribution combines shared site contact details, company imagery, domain records, and an employee photo showing Facebook page administration. This is stronger evidence than a shared page name or template alone. [2:14–3:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GEHHTh5OTnQ&t=134s). |

The broader MEO study adds evidence of synchronized page creation/renaming, recycled story templates, engagement prompts, paid audience acquisition, and apparently taken-over celebrity pages. It also observes publishing moving between domains. These are findings about MEO’s larger network, not proof of every HTV account’s practices. The study documents automated advertising exchanges funding the websites and reports that most posts attract little reaction, with scale and occasional viral posts producing the aggregate reach. [MEO report, pp. 5–13](https://mediatechdemocracy.com/files/publications/facebook-news-slop-economy_2026.pdf).

My interpretation: the main commercial advantage is a repeatable distribution and publishing operation. AI lowers production cost; the pages acquire attention; websites convert visits into advertising inventory. This does not establish profitability, advertiser intent, or a political sponsor.

The sources do **not** establish HTV’s monthly revenue, profit, ad budget, click-through rate, revenue per thousand visits, exact publishing schedule, LLM provider, prompts, or automation software. A software name in a sample website article is not proof that the company uses that software. No evidence here establishes use of MoneyPrinterTurbo or Codex.
